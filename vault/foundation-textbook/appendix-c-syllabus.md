Appendix C - The Foundation Certificate in Cider and Perry Production Syllabus


## Introduction


This qualification is designed to provide a broad appreciation of the main types and styles of cider and perry, knowledge of the cider industry, its marketplace & the structures in which it operates and essential knowledge & understanding of the main principles and practices of cider & perry production. Who it is aimed at The qualification is intended as primary vocational training in cider & perry production, along with those who wish gain a broad appreciation & understanding about the products concerned. As such, the qualification is suitable for those wishing to commence cider & perry production, new entrants into the cider industry & for those already involved in specific aspects of work &/or other sectors of the drinks industry (e.g. laboratory staff, brewers & wine makers) & who wish to broaden their knowledge & understanding about cider & perry. Entry Requirements Candidates applying to sit the examination must be over the legal minimum age for the retail purchase of alcoholic beverages. Key Aims The key aims of this qualification are to enable the individual to: 1. Develop a broad appreciation of the main types & styles of cider and perry, along with an understanding of the Cider Industry - including its historical development & its position in the alcoholic drinks market. 2. Gain knowledge & understanding of the main principles, practices & methods of cider production. 3. Develop basic practical skills involved in cider & perry production & in the control & assurance of product quality. 4. Explore ways in which to encourage the positive & responsible development of the industry & its products. Learning Methods

- Minimum 30 hours tutor led learning;

- Minimum 12 hours independent study of the qualification textbook, along with other relevant material;

- Tutored organoleptic assessment of a minimum of 4 different styles of products.

Guidance for Tutors and Students Each of the main sections of the syllabus – as defined by the four Learning Outcomes – is weighted. The weighting is defined as ‘a notional measure of the substance of the section’ & will be reflected in the proportion of each in the final assessment. The percentage figure also indicates the balance of delivery and learning & therefore provides an estimate of time that should be allocated to direct teaching, instruction & supported individual study. It excludes learner-initiated private study. Examination and Assessment To gain this qualification, the candidate should successfully complete:

- A 90 minute examination made up of 20 multi-choice and 20 short answer questions;

- A portfolio of organoleptic assessment notes for at least 6 products to confirm a knowledge & understanding of a

range of different styles of cider & perry. Sample examination questions are included at the end of each section of the syllabus as below.

Learning Outcomes Outcome A: Explore and examine the nature of cider & perry (Weighting: 15%) Performance Criteria 1. Definitions, standards & characteristics of cider & perry are investigated & stated/summarised. 2. The main terminology used to describe cider & perry are defined & explained. 3. The main categories, types and styles of cider and perry are identified & characterized. 4. The key attributes of cider and perry are characterized, the main strengths & weaknesses of the product - along with potential future threats & opportunities for the category - are explored & trends in new product development are identified. 5. The effect of alcohol on the human body and its impact on society is outlined & methods to encourage the responsible consumption of cider & perry are explored & identified. 6. Ways in which cider & perry can be stored & served are explored & identified. Outcome B: Explore the history, heritage & traditions of cider & perry production & examine the structure, scale & organisation of the modern-day cider market & industry. (Weighting: 10%) Performance Criteria 1. The origins of cider production are explored, the factors which have influenced the location & development of cider / perry manufacture throughout the world are characterized & the history of the industry in North America is outlined. 2. The size, structure, diversity & development of the current cider market & industry are outlined & comparisons made between different counties & regions of the world. 3. Heritage and traditions associated with cider / perry production are investigated & appraised. 4. Opportunities to build on and exploit the industry’s history & heritage are identified & explored. 5. The legislative framework within which the industry operates is outlined. 6. Characteristics of other alcoholic beverages are compared with that of cider & perry. 7. The function, role, aims & objectives of industry organizations are summarized. The role of the industry promoting & encouraging social responsibility, environmental sustainability & support of the rural economy is summarised. Sample multiple-choice examination question: According to the current definition of Cider as detailed in the Canadian Food and Drug Regulations (C.R.C., c. 870; Section B.02.120 [S]. Cider), which one of the following statements is correct? A. Cider is the product of the alcoholic fermentation of apple juice. It should contain not less than 2.5% ABV & not more than 13.0% ABV. B. Cider should contain not less than 1.2% ABV & not more than 8.5% ABV. C. Cider should contain not less than 1.2% ABV & not more than 13.0% ABV, with a level of carbonation less than 3.92 grams per liter CO2. D. None of the above. Sample short-answer question:

- In the context of a SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) analysis of cider in North America, identify &

explain ONE Strength, ONE Weakness & One Threat. Sample multiple-choice examination question: What do you understand by “Wassailing”? A. A type of dance undertaken by English West Country Morris Men to celebrate the tapping of the first barrel of cider in the early Spring of each year B. A traditional South African cider drinking custom to celebrate the first fish caught on a sailing trip off the coast of the country. “Was” is the Afrikaans’ translation of “fish”. C. A traditional ceremony, usually carried out around the New Year, to salute the apple trees & protect them from evil spirits D. A traditional Spanish ceremony associated with pouring cider into a thin-walled cider glass. “Wassailing” can be literally translated as “Throwing the Cider”. Sample short-answer question:

- Briefly describe how ONE specific named aspect of the heritage & traditions associated with the historical development of

cider industry in North America could be used to promote the industry & its products in the 21st century

Outcome C: Through sensory evaluation, demonstrate a broad knowledge and understanding of the main types & styles of cider and perry available in the market place. (Weighting: 25%) Performance Criteria 1. The key stages & requirements involved in the planning, preparation & execution of sensory assessment of cider/ perry are summarised & methods used in the industry are outlined. 2. The techniques involved in sensory assessment are described & carried out. 3. The key sensory components & attributes of cider & perry are identified & described & potential sensory faults are characterized. 4. A portfolio of organoleptic assessment notes is assembled to confirm knowledge & understanding of the main types & styles of cider & perry. Outcome D: Demonstrate knowledge & understanding of the main stages & processes involved in the production of cider & perry & the factors that affect product quality. (Weighting: 50%) Performance Criteria 1. The main principles of apple & pear orcharding are summarised & the main categories of apples & pears used for cider & perry making are stated & examples provided. 2. Other main ingredients & processing aids of cider & perry are identified & their use is described. 3. The key principles underpinning the cider/perry production process are explained. Key principles include: Materials compatible for use in cider production; microbial control & the maintenance of appropriate standards of hygiene; Quality Assurance. 4. The main processes used in the production of cider & perry are outlined. Main processes include: Harvesting & processing of the raw materials; juice preparation; microbial control; fermentation; racking; maturation & blending; processing & packaging for the marketplace. 5. A range of different methods & techniques that can be utilized in the production of cider & perry are identified & outlined & main factors that affect the choice of each are explained. Methods & techniques include: Fruit selection; use of press aids & enzymes; pH control; use of SO2 & other preservatives; yeast selection, nutrition & management; oxygen & temperature control; malo-lactic fermentation; blending & product make-up including choice of ingredients; product clarification methods; packaging choice; methods for in-pack stabilization & maintenance of shelf-life. 6. The main function & categories of equipment involved in the production of cider/perry from the raw materials through to final processing & packaging are described & factors that affect the choice of specific equipment are explained along with the advantages & disadvantages of each different type. Equipment includes: Mills & presses; tanks & vessels; pumps & pipelines; filtration equipment; pasteurizers; packaging. 7. The main factors that affect product quality are identified & a range of potential microbiological & chemical faults are characterized & described along with an explanation of the preventative methods that can be utilized. 8. A range of key techniques & methods involved in laboratory analysis (including SG. Total Acidity, pH, Free & Total SO2), Quality Control & Quality Assurance (including HACCP) are identified & described and their use is explained & justified. Sample multiple-choice examination question: In which one of the following situations is “Analytical” sensory (organoleptic) assessment undertaken? A. Carried out by a consumer group to find out how much they like or dislike a particular product B. Carried out by a consumer group to find out which, out of a range of products, they prefer C. Carried out by the Chief Executive, Managing Director or owner of a cider company to establish which cider or perry he or she prefers D. Carried out by trained and experienced staff to identify any potential off-flavors in cider or perry Sample short-answer question:

- For TWO different types (styles) of cider, identify & explain the most important sensory (organoleptic) properties that

distinguishes one from the other.

Jointly, as a preservative to control the growth of unwanted micro-organisms & as an anti-oxidant to extend the shelf- life of the packaged product.
