# Perry Production

> Source: Peter Mitchell, *Cider & Perry Production — A Foundation* (2nd ed., 2021), Cider Institute of North America. C. Making Cider & Perry — Stage One: Fruit Selection to Maturation, pages 49–49.

Essentially the production of perry is the same as for cider; however there are certain differences and the following points need to be borne in mind:

- If possible, only use ‘proper’ perry pears. If using dessert pears, better results may be obtained by using pear

concentrate (PJC) instead of fresh pear juice, as the evaporation process involved in making PJC removes some of the potential off-flavoured volatile components found in some pears.

- Some varieties of pears require immediate processing after harvest, others will benefit from a significant period of

storage. It is often very difficult to judge the ideal time for processing – you may only have a 24-hour ‘window’ during which time the pears are just right for processing. Pears rot from the inside, therefore rotting can be difficult to spot.

- Whereas apples float, pears sink in water, therefore your water handling system may need modification

- Pears can contain considerable quantities of un-fermentable sorbitol, which will often result in “dry” fermented

perry having a residual high S.G. and tasting quite sweet. With respect to initial estimates for alcohol concentration after fermentation and calculations for sugar additions for possible chapitalisation if required, estimate that the fully attenuated perry will be at a final SG of 1.005. Sorbitol is also a laxative and some people are quite sensitive to its effects. In some countries of the world, there is a requirement to label-declare the presence of sorbitol.

- Pears contain considerable quantities of citric acid which can be converted anaerobically to acetic acid by Lactic

Acid Bacteria. Thus it is usually recommended to completely avoid MLF in perry.

- Pears also contain considerable quantities of acetaldehyde, which will bind SO2. Therefore you need to add

additional SO2 to the juice for it to be effective - add an extra 50mgl-1 over and above the amount you would normally add to apple juice prior to fermentation and to cider.

- Perry from perry pears can be difficult to clear and can be very astringent due to the presence of complex phenolic

compounds. Perry will often respond well to gelatin fining.

- Perry pears from long-established traditional orchards often contain very low levels of nitrogen therefore will often

require routine supplementation.

- Due to the typical subtle, but complex flavours typically found in perry, these sensory properties can suffer badly

even from slight oxidation or the presence of any other organoleptic faults. Cider & Perry Maturation Vats
