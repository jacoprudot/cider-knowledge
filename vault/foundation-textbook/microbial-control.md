# Microbial Control — Building Design, Sanitization & SO₂

> Source: Peter Mitchell, *Cider & Perry Production — A Foundation* (2nd ed., 2021), Cider Institute of North America. C. Making Cider & Perry — Stage One: Fruit Selection to Maturation, pages 37–38.


## Microbial Control


Overall, the following main aspects require careful thought, attention and action:

- Good hygiene is good business

- Hygienic design & pest control

- Sanitisation

- pH, temperature and oxygen control

- Use of anti-microbial agents e.g. SO2

- Utilization of microbial competition

- Physical treatments – notably pasteurisation and (sterile) filtration

- Continual diligence

The following section provides some key points for consideration with respect to hygienic design of the facility and sanitization of plant and equipment – fundamental aspects that form the foundation for effective microbial control in cider production.


## Building Design


Often premises are not purpose built, however there are steps that can be taken to incorporate good design principles.

- Walls and floors can be made impervious to moisture.

- Cracks can be filled

- Ledges can be removed

- Do not give people anywhere to put rubbish (trash) down except in the correct place. In fact this is not just

applicable for trash, the concept is applicable for everything (equipment, tools, ingredients etc: There should be a correct place for everything and everything should be in its place! Finishes for Floors Impervious: It is important to note that juice & cider are corrosive to concrete. 1:50 - 1:100 drainage slope to drain Tiles Epoxy resin Epoxy paint Finishes for Walls Welded polypropylene sheeting Epoxy resin Epoxy paint Lighting Must be adequate Leave no areas of neglect because if you cannot see it, it will not get cleaned. Pipelines Most cider is moved around by pipe-work, so it is sensible to invest in good quality pipe-work. The major problem with pipe-work is the production of un-drainable legs or sumps. Adequate supports are necessary, dead-legs must be avoided and hygienic couplings should be used (e.g. Tri-clover). Flexible Hoses Typically, flexible hoses are very dangerous because the true extent of damage is hidden. The first thing that happens to a flexi pipe when it comes on site is that someone forces the connector (“hose-tail”) into the end of the hose. Orchard Centre Tank House

This damages the lining and cider immediately gets behind the lining. The lining starts to lift it and a microbial trap is formed. Even if the connector is well applied, the resultant seal is a microbial trap. The only safe connector for a flexible hose is a factory fitted swaged end.


## Sanitization


By definition, this is the WHOLE process involved in achieving a suitable standard of hygiene. It involves CLEANING and DISINFECTION. Detergents & Disinfectants It is important to understand the mode of action of all chemicals and to relate to the generic group. There are only a few main groups: Detergents – used to CLEAN 1. Caustic Typically based on the alkali sodium hydroxide (NaOH), caustic is widely used and does not have any major drawbacks – other than it reacts with CO2, which will reduce the effectiveness of the caustic and can cause a vacuum to be formed in a closed system during Cleaning-in-Place (CIP). Caustic can also strip paint and will split wood, thus it is not suitable for cleaning wooden barrels. 2. Sodium Carbonate is mildly caustic and can be used to clean wooden barrels. 3. Acidic detergents are primarily based on phosphoric & nitric acids are typically used to brighten stainless. Also useful where there is a high concentration of residual CO2 inside a tank. Disinfectants – Used to reduce microbial populations to numbers below which they will not cause problems. 1. Chlorine. Use cold at between 150 – 250mgl-1 chlorine (typically supplied at 9 - 12%v/v - check strength before ordering because if it is cheap it may be weaker!). Relatively cheap and effective … BUT …it can badly affect the flavour of cider at very low concentrations and, to make matters worse, it does not rinse easily. 2. Peroxyacetic Acid (PAA). This is the most widely used disinfectant in the Cider Industry and is also used cold. Although very pungent, it is “food safe” as it breaks down to water and a small amount of acetic acid. Although can be rinsed out with product, it will badly oxidize the cider, therefore it is best rinsed out with clean potable water before product is put into the tank or pipe or equipment that has been treated. It should also be noted that the stock solution supplied deteriorates over time. It is therefore best to use PAA test strips to help achieve the recommended final concentration (usually at 250mgl-1). The contact time is all-important with cold disinfectants. They may kill all known microorganisms, but how long does it take? Twenty minutes is a minimum, longer is much better. Sanitization chemicals can be formulated into:

- Liquids

- Powders

- Foams

- Gels

For tanks and pipe-work, CIP is typically used. Pipe-work is re-circulated with the detergent and tanks are sprayed internally using a “Spray-ball” or “Fury”. A typical sanitization routine may include the following sequence of activities: 1. Removal of gross dirt / debris 2. Water rinse 3. Clean (chemical and physical action) e.g. use a 6%v/v solution of a 30% cold caustic detergent (2.0% w/v as NaOH), with a contact time of at least 20 minutes. 4. Water rinse 5. Disinfect (chemical) Typically dose PAA at 250mgl-1 with a contact time of 30 minutes at an ambient temperature of 10 – 250 C 6. Final rinse (potable water)
