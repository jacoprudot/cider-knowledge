# Juice Composition and the Components of Apple Juice

> Source: Peter Mitchell, *Cider & Perry Production — A Foundation* (2nd ed., 2021), Cider Institute of North America. C. Making Cider & Perry — Stage One: Fruit Selection to Maturation, pages 33–33.

Apple juice is about 90% water and 10% soluble solids. There are a number of different scales used to measure the soluble solid concentration, however by convention Specific Gravity (SG) is the density scale used by cider makers. SG describes the relative density of a liquid compared to pure water, which is given a value of 1.000. As apple and pear juices contain substances in solution, they will have a higher S.G. (typically SG 1.045 – 1.065). It should be noted that alcohol has a lower S.G. than water. Alternatively, the term “Gravity” can be used, which is easily calculated from SG as follows:

- Gravity (Degrees°) = (S.G. x 1000) – 1000. Therefore, apple juice will typically have a gravity of 450 – 650

Degrees Brix and degrees Baume are other scales used to measure the soluble solid concentration of solutions. They are used by wine makers and for juice concentrates. Tables are used to convert between the different scales

- e.g. S.G. 1.040 = 10.0° Brix = 5.60 Baume.

The main components of apple juice are as follows:

- Sugar makes up 80% of the soluble solids and, hence, is the main component that determines the SG of the juice.

Sugars are subsequently converted by the yeast into alcohol during fermentation.
