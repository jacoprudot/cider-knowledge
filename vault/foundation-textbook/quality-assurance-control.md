## What is Quality?


Quality should be achieved by Quality Assurance (QA), which involves planned and systematic actions necessary to provide adequate confidence that the product will satisfy the given requirements. QA requires specifications. QA will incorporate – but should not be based on - Quality Control (QC), which are the day to day operational techniques and activities used to fulfill the requirements for quality. QC involves supervision, monitoring, inspection and undertaking corrective actions if necessary.


## Laboratory Quality Control


A key component of QC is a requirement to undertake laboratory analysis of samples taken at different stages of the whole cider making process. All results thus gained should be considered and acted upon as appropriate. There is no point doing an analysis unless it has a specific use or purpose. Quality Assurance & Control In Cider Production Possibly one of the most important analyses that should be undertaken is in respect to checking that final-product sterile filtration or pasteurisation has been effective. Typically this is carried out, under controlled conditions, by laboratory membrane filtration of, often, a whole bottle sample or a large representative volume (e.g. 500mls) from a bigger pack. Once the product has been filtered, the membrane filter is placed on a suitable agar microbial growth media (e.g. WLN agar; capable of growing yeast, bacteria and moulds) and incubated at a warm temperature (280 C) for about 4 days. This is a so-called “presence or absence test”. Obviously, a negative result should be expected, but if microorganisms are found then remedial action will be necessary.


## Record Keeping


As an essential component of cider making, the key features of a record keeping system are that:

- It should be usable – records should only be kept for a purpose

- It should allow for full traceability of the product

- It should be easy to operate and simple

- It should be unambiguous and clearly understandable – much of the data collected is a record of an actual

parameter therefore recognized (S.I.) units should be used.

- It should incorporate accountability – e.g. signed by the individual concerned

- Training should be provided – not just associated with the system itself but as to why details are being recorded


## Quality Assurance (QA)


Quality Assurance is THE key to Successful Cider Making and, as indicated above, it is based on Prevention NOT cure: By firstly recognising the things that may possibly go wrong, a preventative plan is developed and controls implemented and operated. Thus by controlling the whole process and all its steps, you assure (“make certain”) that there will be quality. There really are no problems in cider making as they are all preventable! QA involves:

- Planning: It’s all in the planning!

- Operating effective preventative measures – i.e. Controls

- Continual learning & responding to what happens - Striving for continual improvement

As outlined in Section A above and in most countries of the world there is a legal obligation to operate a food quality management system based on the principles of Hazard Analysis Critical Control Point (HACCP). This system is the most widely known and adopted QA system in the food and drink sector and, as such, focuses attention on assuring consumer safety. HACCP is built on an initial foundation of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP), termed as a “Pre- Requisite Program”, which involves having a range of generic controls operating throughout the business. These include the following:

- Supplier assessment (i.e. only use reputable suppliers)

- Pest control

- Personal hygiene (e.g. hand-washing after going to the bathroom)

- Staff training

- Waste management

- Cleaning schedules

- Customer complaints procedures

- Preventative maintenance

Although not a legal requirement – but good business practice - the actual process involved in developing, implementing and operating a HACCP Plan can also can also be extended to product quality issues (e.g. the prevention of off-flavours being produced) and process effectiveness (e.g. avoidance of stuck fermentations).
