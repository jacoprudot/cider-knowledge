## Why carry out sensory analysis?


Hedonic Assessment: A measure of relative & absolute likes and dislikes.

- Will be subjective / biased

- Tests should be objective / representative and carried out in a statistically controlled manner

- Carried out by the CONSUMER

- Includes “Acceptance” and “Ranking” tests

Analytical Assessment: A range of objective, non-judgmental techniques, undertaken by trained panelists. Includes:

- Difference (discrimination) testing – “Are the products different? e.g. Triangle Test. Can include testing for the

degree of difference – Ranking Tests (samples placed in order of the intensity of a specified criterion).

- Scaling – A scale of intensity used to describe the degree of stimulus e.g. level of sweetness.

- Descriptive Analysis – Characterization of the sensory properties e.g. use of a Flavour Wheel, detection of

off-flavours. Process best developed through the use of “Free-Choice” profiling, followed by group discussion which then aims to relate derived descriptors to flavour wheels – defined & agreed standards / references / descriptors For Descriptive Analysis, non-judgmental and agreed/standardized terminology should be used. Terms should be used unambiguously and correctly, which when given individual intensity-scores, can lead to full flavor profiles of products being developed. This type of analysis is also used to identify off-flavors, to compare different raw materials and different processes and – importantly – to help the cider maker understand what the consumer likes and dislikes.


## Use of Sensory Analysis in Industry


There are two main uses of sensory analysis in the cider industry, which are summarised below. 1. Monitoring and Quality Control. In addition to routine laboratory testing, the following sensory analysis work is typically undertaken:

- Assessment of raw materials and ingredients;

- On-going evaluation of the products throughout the whole of the

production process;

- Characterization of the final products including any deviation from

specification;

- Detection of any off flavors;

- An evaluation of any consumer complaints;

- Establishment and monitoring of product shelf life.

## Implementation of Sensory Analysis


A number of aspects should be borne in mind when planning to undertake any formal sensory analysis of ciders and perries. Consideration should be given to the environment in which the work is to be carried out, the resources required and the conduct of the sensory analysis itself. A. The Environment o Protect from noise, odours and other potentially interfering stimuli. o Aim for a stable room temperature of about 20 – 220 C. o Ideally, walls, floors, ceilings should be non-absorbent and pale & neutral colors to be used. If possible, avoid curtains, carpets etc. o Lighting should be even and shadow-free (Natural, incandescent). o Individual booths provide the best environment for many sensory tests, although a round table is used for interactive tasting. o A sample preparation area should ideally be separated from the tasting area. B. The Resources and Equipment Required o Serving of samples should be made in glass, not plastic – a tulip-shaped glass is ideal to assess aroma. o Use an odorless detergent for glass washing (or none at all). Rinse thoroughly. Store in an odor free environment and re-wash if stored for longer than one day. o Unsalted crackers and “Table” or filtered water should be available. o Spittoons should be provided. C. Test Conduct q Carry tests out late morning or afternoon q Serve 50 - 100ml of sample at: o 120 - 16°C (for flavor). o 80 - 10°C (for mouthfeel). o Drinking temperature (for consumer preference or acceptance tests). q Do not present too many samples in a single session. For example: o 10 - 12 in a pass-fail test. o 8 for paired test. o 4 samples for descriptive testing (2 if strong flavored). q Provide clear instructions to Assessors with respect to: o The test procedure. o The score-sheet design. o The type of response required. q Maintain motivation in assessors. q Assessors should not: ✗ Use odorous cosmetics or soap on the day of the test. ✗ Smoke or eat / drink anything 30 minutes before the test. ✗ Be involved in testing if they suffer from colds, have had recent dental treatment or are suffering from emotional stress.
